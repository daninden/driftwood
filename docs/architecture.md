# Architecture of Driftwood

Driftwood is small and lonely: one island, one castaway, atmosphere first (see `design.md`). The architecture has to keep it that way. Every part has one job, can be tested on its own, and knows as little as possible about the rest.

## Layers

```
core/     numbers and noise           no dependencies
world/    the island as data          uses core (and only the curve maths of three)
game/     rules and game state        uses core and world
render/   everything you see          uses core, world (read only) and three
audio/    everything you hear         uses core, world (read only), game state (read only) and render's pure maths (wind, water breath); Web Audio only in audio/sound
main.js   wires it all together       the only place that knows every layer
heightWorker.js, parallelHeights.js   build the height grids in Web Workers for main (use core and world)
```

Rules:

- **Arrows point one way.** `world` does not know `game` or `render`. `game` does not know `render`. `render` reads the world and the player state but never changes them. `audio` is presentation like `render`: it reads the world and the game and changes neither; `render` does not know `audio`.
- **`world` and `game` are pure.** They touch neither the DOM nor WebGL, so they run in Node (the tests) and in Web Workers (the height grids, see Loading).
- **Everything follows from the seed.** The same number gives the same island, the same trees and the same colours. Every tile has its own generator (seed, tx, tz), so the order in which tiles are built does not matter.
- **Almost no interface.** There is deliberately no HUD, map or menu. What you see is the island. The marks on screen are the moon that shows how rested the castaway is (`render/restMark`), next to it the drop that shows how thirsty he is (`render/thirstMark`) and the half coconut that shows how hungry he is (`render/hungerMark`), both in `#needs` and the row of what he carries loose (`render/carryMark`: fibre, branches and, since 0.22, crabs), top left the line with his name and the day (`render/nameLines`), and on touch screens the thumb stick and (everywhere) the action button (`render/thumbStick`, `render/actionButton`); all in one ink colour that turns pale at night (`render/ink`). The one exception is the developer panel (`render/devPanel`), a faint line top right with version, name, fps, work time per frame and `idle`; it is a developer aid, visible for now, to be hidden later (`DEV_PANEL_VISIBLE`), and toggled with the backtick key.

## Modules

**Hands (0.29.13):** `game/hands` is a pure, browser-local preference and controller for `self`, `computer` and `auto`: safe `driftwood-hands` storage, H key guard and the decision whether the mind drives. It is never part of an island save. `main` combines Auto with `game/input`'s 30-second timer; Computer drives immediately, and a real player walk/action changes it to Self. `KeyH`, camera, sound, quality and developer controls remain passive.

**Tideline (0.30.13):** `game/mind` has no drying pile. Wet driftwood from the strip (`SRC.strip`) is carried a few metres up the beach by `pickUpWet` + `layTideline` (spot: `tideSpot`) and laid just above the high-water line with the game's own `lay`; the numbers are the `TIDE_*` constants. Its identity set (`rescued`) is for the statistics only, not saved game state. The daily round (`tideRound`, its own PRNG `rngTide`, `TIDE_ROUND_*`) is called from `idleGoal` after the morning home work and before exploring: the nearest known strip with wet wood (`tideTarget`, by path cost on known ground), home before dark by the exploring trip's `exploreLate`, up to two loads. `TIDE_CAMP` is 11 m: a pile nearer the fire could land where the lean-to's site, its ring or its upgrade piles come, and with 6 m that left several lives without a site. With no spot at hand (he stands in the camp) wet wood in his arms is carried to the nearest known strip (`stripPoint`) and laid up the beach there, never as a pile at the camp. A pile is only a stop of a fetch with empty arms (the game offers no pickup from a pile with wood under his arm). Firewood fetches (`nearestBy`, `selectStops`) weigh a dry pile at a beach edge that is not his own by `TIDE_BONUS` (0.7) times its walking cost.

**Voice (0.30.1 "Thoughts"):** `game/game` retains its optional accepted-action notification subscription. `game/mind` emits situation-led `onIntent` notifications before need goals and actual material, feeding and lighting errands, once per thought kind per goal. Since 0.30.20 "Words" actions never speak: `main` no longer wires `game.onAction` to the voice (the subscription stays in `game/game` for other listeners), and `render/voice` `PHRASES` holds intent kinds only (3 or 4 lines each, a per-kind rotation: `phraseFor(kind, n)` returns line `n` modulo the count and `createThoughts` counts per kind, so a kind never repeats a line in a row; never a PRNG). New intents in `game/mind` (`think` once per kind per goal, at the start of an errand): `spearIntent` (`makeSpearTrip`), `fishIntent` (`fishTrip` as the afternoon pastime), `roastIntent` (`roastCatch`), `homeWoodIntent` (home material in `gather`), `buildIntent` / `upgradeIntent` (`doBuild`), `torchIntent` (`torchFor`), and per journey phase `journeyIntent`, `campIntent`, `homeIntent` (`sayOnce`, tracked in `journey.said` per phase and night). The old `drytransfer` phrase is gone: no step of the mind carries dry wood to the fire's pile for that reason any more (0.29.5). The player's need thoughts: `game/lack` (`lackOf`, pure; `LACK_KINDS`) and `game.lackAt(x?, z?)` read the very conditions of the 'fish' and 'light' actions and return 'needSpear', 'spearAway', 'needFibre', 'needMoreWood' or null; `main` asks once per still period (about 1.5 s without walk, movement, work or sleep, the mind not driving) and gives a kind to `voice.announce`, under the same 8 s / 60 s limits. `render/voice` owns fixed phrases, its independent counter and a presentation-only quiet-time gate (8 seconds globally, 60 per kind; no deferred queue). `voice.hold(until)` (0.30.8 "Morning") holds the projected line back while the wake line shows: `main` calls `voice.hold(now + WAKE_TIME)` where it calls `wakes.show` and gives `voice.update` the same steady clock (`performance.now() / 1000`), so the hold ends exactly when the wake line has faded; a line accepted meanwhile is logged at once and displayed when the hold ends (the 8 s gate means at most one can wait through the 5 s wake line; there is no queue). It stores only displayed entries, prefixed with `voiceTime(game.clock.time)` as HH:mm without dates, while the projected fading line remains unprefixed. No callback changes decisions, chance streams, save state or need thresholds. `main` also wires passive L; the log is never saved or stored.

### Mind errands (0.29.8)

`game/mind` keeps an internal errand depth while a concrete material task is running: gathering or delivering fire/store/home material, husking for supplies, or a crab trip. The priority check still runs every `CHECK` seconds and all existing goal `LIMIT`s still end a stuck errand, but ordinary higher-ranked drink, eat and sleep goals wait until that generator returns. `errandPreempts` is pure and allows the two emergency overrides only: drink at `VERY_AT` (1 day of thirst) and eat at `VERY_HUNGER_AT` (2 days of hunger). Idle rests and strolls never enter the errand scope.

Material lookup combines loose finds with eligible piles on known ground and compares their `game/path` cost. A pile is eligible only when its top carry-sized group satisfies the source's rules (dry driftwood for the fire); fire and lean-to-site piles are excluded (a pile at a beach edge is a find). The player's normal pickup still takes the top group, so the mind follows the same stack order.

Test hooks: `mind.axePlan`, `mind.logDest`, `mind.axeSpot`, `mind.axeSnap`. Tests: `tests/clearing.test.js`.

## One frame

The loop runs at the screen's rate but `main` only runs a frame when `render/framePacer` says it is due: at most 30 a second on touch-only devices (`touchOnly` from `render/thumbStick`), none on a desktop, and at most 15 a second on every device once nothing has been happening for 3 s. Happening: the castaway moving, working, bending, lying down or asleep (the sped-up sleep keeps the normal rate), the camera still easing (`view.settling`), a key held, the stick, a walking target, the action button held, and any pointer, touch, key or wheel event (listened for on `window` in the capture phase, so the stick counts too; an event while idle also drops the schedule, so the next screen frame is drawn at once). Fire and torch flicker and the slow sun and tide do not count. The game's `dt` is the time since the last drawn frame, clamped to 0.05 s (0.1 s while idle, so the game clock does not run slow at 15 fps). `__driftwood.frames` counts the drawn frames.

**Test speed (`?speed=N`, 0.30.11 "Hurry", developer and tester only).** N is clamped to 1..16 (`game/steps`). Per drawn frame `main` runs `game.update` (and `mind.step` when the habit drives) once per sub-step of `subSteps(dt, speed)`, so the game gets `N * dt` in steps of at most 0.1 s, the same step as the simulations; the distances walked are summed for the footprints, and `view.render` still runs once per frame. On the faster clock: the game and the mind, the takeover's quiet wait (`tnow()` = real time plus the time gained) and everything the game derives from `game.clock` (weather, wind state, the tide, footprint age, the day line). On real time: the drawing and every pure-visual timer (`view.render`'s dt: camera easing, water, wind sway, fire flicker, wildlife, mood), the sound, the UI fades (`show`, wake line, voice lines), the autosave and view-saver timers, the dev panel and the quality governor. The speed is not saved and the island saves as usual; the dev panel shows `· ×N` after the work time when N > 1. `__driftwood.step` stays at 1/60 s steps.

## Rain

Rain (0.21 "Rain") is a pure schedule (`game/weather`, from the seed and the clock; no state, nothing in the save for it) and an intensity that `game.update` copies to `game.rain` each frame. Two things follow in the game layer: `wood.rain(total, falling, sheltered, player)` (about every 0.4 s of game time, and only while it falls or when something is still wet) sets `log.t = now` on the carried wood, every pile, all washed-up wood and the other loose wood within `RAIN_REACH` (40 m) of him that no finished lean-to covers (0.22.1: with the optional `lastFall` from `game/weather`, loose wood in the open that comes within reach is caught up as of the end of the last shower (carried wood and piles only while it falls, as they can have been moved), if less than its drying time has passed, so far wood is wetted lazily and a shower he slept through counts; a branch is skipped if it grew after that, `g`; the shelter check uses the lean-tos that exist at catch-up time), so the existing dryness mechanism (`dryness(log, total)`, with `BRANCH_DRY_DAYS` half a day for branches) dries it again from the moment the rain stops; start wood and branches that got wet are kept in the optional `wet` field of the wood's save (wood washed up since the start already carries its `t`). `fire.update(days, rain, at, total)` takes an optional `rain(x, z)` test (true where no lean-to covers within 2 m): fires there burn `RAIN_BURN` times as fast, count `rained` days (optional field of the fire) and go out at `RAIN_OUT` (fifteen game minutes) as wet ash (`burnt` with `wet: true`, a puff of steam, `hissed` and `hissAt` for the sound); torches burn `TORCH_RAIN` (2) times as fast. The render and the sound only read `game.rain`: `islandView.render` passes it to the mood, the water, the ground uniform and the rain layer; `main` passes it to the mixer together with whether he stands under a lean-to. To see a shower: `?time=` with a whole day in front sets the clock (`?time=8.1`, see `docs/handover.md`).

## Sound

Sound (0.20 "Sound") lives in `audio/`, beside `render`. `main` builds `createMixer(world)` and `createSound()`; each drawn frame, once sound is running, it hands the mixer the player, the terrain info of the frame, the sea level, the day factor, the wind state (the same `windState` the picture uses, from `view.windTime`), the water clock (`view.waterTime`, so the surf breathes with the water's edge), `game.fire`, `game.wildlife.birds()` and the number of carried pieces, and gives the result to `sound.update`. The first key, tap or click calls `sound.gesture()` (capture phase on `window`, so answering the name question counts); M, or a tap on the mark, calls `setEnabled`. A rain layer for 0.21 has its gain node in the graph and a `rain` target (0) in the mix; adding rain means a noise chain into `L.rain` and a number in `mixOf`.

Cost: the mixer takes about 0.002 ms a frame, the look round him (about four times a second) 0.04 ms, and `sound.update` about 0.03 ms on this machine (mostly `setTargetAtTime` calls; one-shot events add a few small nodes each). The audio itself runs on the browser's audio thread. While sound is off or the tab is hidden the context is suspended and `update` is not called.

## Quality

`main` picks the start level with `pickStart` (`?quality=N` forces a level and stops adapting, for tests; otherwise the level remembered in `localStorage` key `driftwood-quality`, one per device, not per island; else 0) and passes `settingsFor(level, { touch })` to `createIslandView`. After the island has faded in a governor (`createGovernor`) is fed, every drawn frame, the real time since the previous frame and whether the pacer idles. It measures the interval, not the work time, because the work time leaves out the graphics card; on a touch device capped at 30 a healthy interval is 33 ms, still under the 37 ms limit. When it steps down, `main` calls `view.setQuality(settings)` (`stage.applyQuality`), shows `qN` in the dev panel and saves the level (not with the test parameters `?spawn`, `?time`, `?awake`). `__driftwood.quality` gives the level. `npm run smoke -- --quality=N` runs the smoke test with a forced level.

The wind (0.19) is on at every level: it adds a few multiply-adds per vertex of the plants on the detailed tiles, in the picture pass and in the shadow pass, which measured within the noise of a frame (about 10 ms at the first frames, 7 ms later, 1 ms settled on the desktop card, before and after alike), so no level turns it off or turns the shadow displacement off. The wind clock (`view.windTime`, `view.setWindTime`) runs with the dt of the drawn frames, so it needs no game clock and does not race during a sped-up sleep; `__driftwood.draw` sets it to 20 s before drawing, so that every smoke shot has the wind at the same moment.

## Loading

Loading takes about 3 seconds on a 4-core laptop. The height grids are the heaviest part (8.3 million points): `main.js` has them built in Web Workers, one per spare core (`src/heightWorker.js` computes strips of rows with `world/heightfield` `fillRows`, `src/parallelHeights.js` hands out the strips and puts the grids together). The result is exactly what a build on the main thread gives (tested); if workers are not available, `main` falls back to that. Then `islandView` builds the water and only the far tiles around the start (the others follow while playing), and `view.prepare` builds the detailed tiles in view at the start, so the game never opens on the simple stand-in tiles. A detailed tile costs about 0.15 s: its ground colour is sampled every second pixel and blended in between, and its 21 000 brush strokes are painted straight into the pixel data (`paintStroke`) rather than through the canvas API. `main.js` lets the browser breathe between the steps and fills the loading bar from the step reports (`core/loading`). When the first frame is drawn, the loader fades out and the island fades in.

Nothing stutters while playing, because detailed tiles are built in slices of about 9 ms per frame and the remaining far tiles in slices of about 3 ms.

## Publishing

The game is published on Vercel as a static site. The Vercel project is linked to the GitHub repository: every push to `main` is built there with `npm run build` and goes live from `dist/` (see `vercel.json`). So `main` is always what players see; run `npm test` (and for visual changes the smoke test) before pushing.

## Saving

Built in 0.6: one automatic save per island in the browser's `localStorage`, key `driftwood-save-<seed>`.

- **The world is never saved.** It follows from the seed. What is saved is `game.state()`, a plain object: the seed, the time and day, the player's position and heading, how long he has been awake and the sleep in progress, the wood (carried, piles, washed up, lost, the last high water that brought wood), the fibre and coconuts taken, the fires, burnt places, torches and attempts, thirst and hunger and (0.22) the crabs carried and caught. Not saved: the view, footprints, kneeling work and a pending tap.
- **Restoring is `createGame(world, spawn, saved)`.** The options of `createGame` are the shape of `state()`, so each part (`createClock`, `createWood`, `createSleep`, `createFibre`, `createFire`) starts from its own saved state, and `state()` of the restored game gives back the same object (tested). A saved position stays exactly where it was when walking can leave him there (`game/movement` `canOccupy`, 0.27.3: against a trunk, on a steep step of a climbing route). A saved position he cannot be at any more (the island changed: a river now deep, a new boulder) is moved to the nearest spot he can (`game/movement` `nearestStandable`: rings of 0.5 m steps up to 15 m, using the walking rules via `canStand`), else to the start spot.
- **`src/save.js`** is the browser glue next to `main`: pure `serialise`, `parseSave` (format number `v: 1`, same seed, shape checked; anything else gives `null`, a fresh start, never a crash), `momentKey`, `parseHash`, `withoutNew`, `isResetKey`, and `createSaveStore(seed)` with `load`, `save`, `clear`, each wrapped in try/catch so blocked storage just means no saving. A save with another format number is ignored (and overwritten by the next save); the number goes up when the format changes incompatibly.
- **When it saves** (`main`): every 10 s of play, when `momentKey` changes (a pile gets wood or loses it, a fire is lit or goes out, a torch is planted or taken up, he falls asleep or wakes), and on `visibilitychange` (hidden) and `pagehide`. With `?spawn`, `?time` or `?awake` (the test parameters; `?speed` is not one, and saving works as usual) nothing is loaded or saved, and the name question is skipped.
- **The view (0.10):** the format stays `v: 1`. A save may carry an optional `view: { yaw, tilt, zoom }` (camera `pose`: the turn wrapped into 0..2π, the tilt and zoom the player's goals, not the eased values). `parseSave` makes it safe with `clampView` (tilt 25°..45°, zoom 5..25; the camera then clamps to 10 unless the dev panel is visible) and drops a malformed one while keeping the rest of the save. `main` calls `view.setPose` right after the island view is created, before `view.prepare` chooses the tiles, so the first frame is already in the saved pose; a new start, `?new`, an old save or the test parameters leave the default view (turned 45°, tilted 30°, zoom 7). It is saved with every save and, through `createViewSaver`, once 1.5 s after the view stopped changing.
- **Crabs (0.22):** the format stays `v: 1`. A save may carry `crabs: { carried: [total], gone: [[crabId, back]], digs }` (the crabs he carries with the clock day at which each was caught, at most 2; the caught crabs with the day they return to their colony; the number of digs, which fixes the next dig's luck); optional, older saves load with nothing carried and nothing gone, and a wrong type makes the save unusable. `momentKey` also counts the crabs carried and gone, so a catch, a meal or a spoiled crab is saved at once. What the crabs themselves do (out, hiding) is still not saved.
- **Spear and fish (0.30.17):** the format stays `v: 1`. A save may carry `spear: { hand: boolean, at: { x, z } | null }` and `fish: { carried: [total], tries }` (at most 2 fish; `tries` fixes the next try's luck); both optional, an older save loads with no spear and no fish, and a wrong type makes the save unusable like any other field. `momentKey` also counts the fish carried and the spear's state (none, in his hand, lying), so a catch, a meal, a spoiled fish, or the spear made, taken up or laid down is worth a save.
- **Shells and nuts (0.25):** the format stays `v: 1`. A save may carry `shells: { full: [boolean], nuts }` (the half shells kept, at most 2, each full of water or not; the coconuts carried, 0 to 2); optional, older saves load with none, and a wrong type (more than 2, a non-boolean, a fractional or negative count) makes the save unusable. `momentKey` counts them too, so a kept, filled or drunk shell and a taken or eaten nut are saved at once.
- **Inland (0.26):** the format stays `v: 1`. A save may carry `berries` and `bananas`, each `{ carried: [total], picked: [[plantId, total]] }` (at most 2 and 1 carried with the moment each was picked, and the plants picked with the day, for their regrowing, `game/forage`); optional, older saves have none (nothing carried, nothing picked). A wrong shape makes the save unusable like any other broken field; ids that are no plant any more and bad numbers are dropped by `createForage`. `momentKey` counts the carried and picked numbers of both.
- **Thirst and burnt places (0.11):** the format stays `v: 1`. A save may carry `thirst: { days }` and `fire.burnt: [{ id, x, z, left, n }]` (at most 64); both are optional, so older saves load as not thirsty with no burnt places, and a wrong type makes the save unusable like any other broken field.
- **Branches and lean-tos (0.16):** the format stays `v: 1`. A save may carry `branches: { taken: [[id, day]], grown?: [[id, day]] }` (0.27.3: `grown` are the branches that grew back in the last 3 days and the day they did, so rain from before they grew stays off them after a load and `wood.wet` is stable over a save; the branches taken from the ground and the day, for their regrowing; ids from `game/branches`) and `leantos: { list: [{ id, x, z, heading, progress }], nextId }` (finished and half built; at most 64). Branches in piles need no field: they are in `wood.piles` with kind `'branch'` and their ids (≥ 2^24; `game/wood` reads them back through `branches.piece`). Both new fields are optional, so older saves load with no branches taken and no lean-tos, and a wrong type makes the save unusable like any other broken field.
- **Shelter upgrades (0.27 "Home"):** the format stays `v: 1`. A save may carry `fronds: { taken: [[id, day]] }` (fronds taken and when, for their regrowing), `stones: { taken: [id] }`, `wood.stones: [id]` (the stones he carries, at most 2), piles of kind `'frond'` or `'stone'`, and on each lean-to `up: { walls, bed, ring }` (progress 0..1; 1 is built). All optional: older saves load without them (no fronds taken, no stones, no upgrades) and a wrong type makes the save unusable. `momentKey` also follows the number of finished upgrades per lean-to.
- **The fire and the stone ring (0.31.1 "Milk"):** `game/leanto` `noFire(x, z)` (the ring of 1 to 3.5 m round a finished ring's middle) is the one rule; `game/game` `lightable` and the finishing of 'light' and 'kindle' ask it, so neither player nor Dave can start a fire there. `game/mind` keeps the stock in the ring (`centreStrays`, the aside pile at `ASIDE_RING`, the pile choice in `lightFire`); no layer changes. Test: `tests/fireRing.test.js`.
- **His habits (0.31.1 "Milk"):** the format stays `v: 1`. A save may carry `habit: { journeyDue, journeyTried, exploredDay, journeys }` (whole numbers, from `mind.state()` of `game/mind`; `main` saves it next to `name` and `startDay` and hands it back as `createMind(world, game, { saved })`). It is not part of `game.state()` (the mind is not game state). Optional: an older save loads as before (the next journey due on day 4, no exploring day); a wrong type makes the whole save unusable like any other broken field; a bad single field inside a good `habit` keeps its start value. The journey in progress is not saved. It is written with every save (every 10 s and at the moments), no new moment.
- **The known ground (0.28 "Habit"):** the format stays `v: 1`. A save may carry `known: { cell: 10, cells: [index] }` (`game/known`: cells of 10 m, index = row * 72 + column, at most 5184, sorted); optional, older saves load without it and the ground round the castaway counts as known. `parseSave` drops a `known` that is not well formed (another cell size, a cell that is not a whole number or outside the island, too many) and still loads the rest of the save.
- **Daily bread (0.29):** the format stays `v: 1`. The sleep state may carry `nap: true` (a nap in progress; missing: not a nap). Old saves load: thirst and hunger above the new limits are clamped to them (collapse and starving), `sleep.awake` above a day and a half makes him fall asleep at once when he is on dry ground.
- **Hunger (0.15):** the format stays `v: 1`. A save may carry `hunger: { days }` (days since he ate, clamped to 0..3 by `game/hunger` since 0.29, was 4), optional like `thirst`: older saves load as not hungry, and a wrong type makes the save unusable like any other broken field.
- **The name (0.9):** the format stays `v: 1`. The save may carry two optional fields beside `game.state()`, added by `main`: `name` (string up to 40 characters; the empty string is an answer, "the castaway") and `startDay` (whole number: the clock's day on which the game started, so day 1). Optional fields need no migration: a save from before 0.9 has neither, so it asks once (`shouldAsk`: no name) and counts days from day 0. A wrong type makes the save unusable like any other broken field. `main` asks while the island loads, waits for the answer before the island fades in, and saves nothing until it has one (a save without a name would count as an answer); starting over clears the save, so the question comes back. The smoke test answers it with an empty Enter.
- **The address (0.30.15 "Address"):** every option is `name=value` after `?`, joined with `&`; the `#` forms are gone. `parseAddress(location.search)` reads `?seed=<n>` and `new` (`?new`, `?seed=2024`, `?seed=2024&new`); the test options (`spawn`, `time`, `awake`, `quality`, `speed`) are read from the same query by `main` and the other modules, unchanged. With `new` the save is cleared at start-up and `new` is removed from the query (`withoutNew`, `history.replaceState`; the other options and any hash stay), so a refresh keeps the game. With `spawn`, `time` or `awake` nothing is loaded or saved. Shift + backtick clears the save and reloads (saving is switched off first, so `pagehide` does not write it again). Plain backtick still toggles the dev panel.
- **A backend later** would sit behind the same `load`/`save`/`clear`, without `game` or `render` noticing.

## Tile rebuilds (0.27.7 "Lighter")

The views of what lies on the ground (`render/driftwood` beach logs, `render/branches`, `render/coconuts`, `render/berries`, `render/bananas`, `render/finds`) keep one group per detailed tile and used to rebuild every near tile when the `version` of their game module went up (a pickup, a regrowth, a drying step). Now each game module (`game/branches`, `game/fibre`, `game/forage` (one per berries and bananas), `game/fronds`, `game/stones`, `game/wood`) also keeps a `dirty` Set of indices into `world.tiles`, filled where a piece is taken or grows back (for `game/wood`: a beach log taken, wood washed up or wetted by rain; piles and what is carried are not tile content), and the view rebuilds only those tiles (`render/tileRebuild`), then empties the set. Changes that touch no tile (a carried handful going bad, a tuft used up) bump `version` and dirty nothing. A view whose module gives no `dirty` rebuilds every held tile, as before. `dirty` is never saved. Driftwood drying: when the drying step changes and some wood can still be wet (`wood.drying`), only the tiles holding a piece with `stillWet` are rebuilt, plus the piles (one group, rebuilt on every wood change). A wash-up that has faded in rebuilds only the tiles that hold its logs. The damp patches of a tile are merged into one mesh per opacity step (`DAMP_BUCKETS`, 8) and share the material of their step (`userData.shared`, kept for the life of the view); the patches of fading wood keep materials of their own.

Other per-frame savings: the container rect is kept (`render/screenRect`), the hero's load list is rebuilt only when a piece or its dry step changes, the carry row compares counts (`carryState`) before it builds its SVG, `main` looks at `momentKey` twice a second (`createMomentWatcher` in `src/save.js`), and the quality governor leaves out frames in which tiles were built.

`scripts/perf.js` (`node scripts/perf.js [--html file] [--out prefix]`, run `npm run build` first) measures the built game in a headless browser: draw calls, JS time of `view.render` per frame (median and 95th percentile) at night beside a fire, among fireflies and by day, the browser's own style and layout time per drawn frame, the frame in which a branch, coconut, berry bunch, banana, frond, stone or beach log is picked up, and the bytes allocated per frame; it also writes night and day screenshots to compare before and after a change.

## Instanced pools (0.27.8 "Bundle")

Before 0.27.8 every tile (near and far) and every ground view made one `InstancedMesh` per kind of small thing, plus a second for its outline hulls: with six near tiles about 430 instanced meshes and 356 to 476 draw calls a frame (shadow pass included). `render/batch` now has pools: `poolsOf(scene)` gives the scene one `PoolSet` (a group `pools` in the scene), and a `Batch` made with it (`new Batch(outlineMat, poolsOf(scene))`) takes slots there instead of making meshes.

- **One pool per kind**: the key is geometry, material, cast and receive flags, depth material, render order, outline material (none when the kind has no hull) and whether the instances carry `aw`. A pool is one `InstancedMesh` and, with a hull, a twin for the outlines (same shape, scaled matrices, pairing and `castShadow` as before). 25 to 30 pools serve the whole near area.
- **Slots**: `B.build(group)` appends a run of slots to each pool it needs, writes matrix, colour, outline matrix and `aWind` there and keeps the runs in `group.userData.slots`. `disposeGroup(group)` (render/nearTile) calls `releaseGroup`, which gives the runs back: the runs after it slide down (typed array `copyWithin`), so the mesh `count` is always the live instances and holes cost no vertex work. Pools grow by doubling (new meshes, copied arrays, old ones disposed; the cloned geometry with `aWind` too) and never shrink; an empty pool is hidden.
- **Uploads**: a change only marks the first changed slot; `pools.flush()` (islandView, once per frame before `renderer.render`) sets the `updateRange` from there to the end, so a tile build uploads the tail of each pool it touched. The instance attributes stay on the default (static) usage: with `DynamicDrawUsage` ANGLE copies them again on every draw and the frame got slower.
- **Culling**: pools are not culled per tile. The streamer already keeps only the tiles in view plus a margin, so what a pool draws is what the view needs. Far tiles are the exception: their instances are in the pools only while the tile is visible (`updateFarTiles`), the rest hold only their ground mesh.
- **Wind**: unchanged. The vertex shader reads the instance matrix (position and phase come from it) and the instanced attribute `aWind`; both are per instance in the pool, and the shadow depth material reads the same, so shadows sway with the plants.
- **Still outside the pools**: the terrain mesh of each tile (its own texture), far and near, the water, the hero, the wildlife, fire, the beach driftwood's damp patches and the fading wood of a wash-up (a Batch without pools makes its own meshes). About 110 of the remaining 175 calls are terrain meshes (main pass plus shadow pass), so draw calls now grow with the number of tiles in view, not with what stands on them.
- `view.stats()` reports `drawCalls` (shadow pass included), `triangles`, `geometries`, `textures`, `meshes`, `instanced`, `instances` and `pools` ({ pools, instances, capacity }); `tests/batch.test.js` covers slots, sliding, growth, outlines, `aWind` and the upload range.

`node scripts/perf.js --only draw [--w 1366 --h 768] [--html file] [--out prefix]` measures this: it walks 100 m inland and back (about 0.12 m a frame) in rounds, standing and zoomed out, then again with the CPU slowed 4x (CDP `Emulation.setCPUThrottlingRate`), and reports draw calls, frame time (median, p95, max; frames in which tiles were built apart), the cost of the tile-building frames, the JS heap after a collection and the `stats()` after every round (the instance and geometry counts must return to the same level).

## Dynamic camp obstacles (0.29.10 "Solid ground")

`game/obstacles` is pure and turns the current piles, fires, burnt places and lean-tos into a read-only nearby-circle callback. Every radius is clearance to the castaway's centre: `game/wood` supplies both `pileVisibleRadius` (the conservative rendered and interaction footprint) and `pileSolidRadius` (the walk-blocking core), and `game/movement` supplies `BODY`. Driftwood and branch piles use the compact 0.45 m core, so their protruding long logs can be stepped over without closing a camp; frond and stone piles retain their visible footprint. A lean-to is a connected run of circles along the full 2 m back and its two 1.5 m sides, never its open front. `game/game` owns that callback and passes it as an option to `game/movement`; `game/mind` gives the same option to `game/path`. Thus `world` does not know game state and there is no circular dependency. (Until 0.30.2 the presence of the `obstacles` callback switched the whole path cache off; 0.30.3 "Routes" restores it by keeping terrain and camp apart, see "Routes (0.30.3)".) Since 0.29.14 the first and final path connections are checked from the exact player and open-goal positions too; dynamic hits win over natural hits, while a person already inside the one obstacle he is leaving keeps movement's escape rule. For a habit fire at a finished ring, `game/mind` approaches at the ordinary pile-drop offset so its existing drop lands exactly at `ringCentre`; player placement is unchanged.

**The walking gap (0.30.10 "Walls").** Found by measuring: with a smaller fire stock (target 6 pieces instead of 12) 5 of 24 ten-day lives, and with feeding only below 0.25 of `FUEL_MAX` 2 of 24, had Dave standing for days at one spot after the walls upgrade (`no-stand:upgrade`, `nopath`, `no-water`), thirst 1.5, collapse. The cause in the reproduced life (seed 2024 walked, stream 2): the second pile of fronds was laid 1.05 m from him over the first one's circle (a frond pile's solid is 1.2 m, the pile spots only asked 1.5 m centre to centre), so two pile circles and the lean-to's wall circles overlapped round his feet; the corridors left were 0 to 0.13 m wide, which the planner (0.05 m margin each side, rings 0.06 m outside) and the stand chooser both count as closed. Planner and stand chooser agreed with the geometry; nothing was wrong in them. The fix is spacing only: `game/obstacles` exports `CAMP_GAP` (`2 * BODY + 2 * 0.05` = 0.5 m, edge to edge; radii already hold `BODY`, so this is the width of the corridor for his centre), `gapFree(camp, x, z, r, skip?)` and `sameFirePlace(x, z)` (a fire place or burnt place under the spot is the same place: a fire's own stock). The walls of a lean-to are exempt (a ring needs solids that touch one another; the upgrade piles stand beside the walls and keep the gap from each other). The rule is applied wherever something is placed: `game/wood` `drop` asks the game's `blockedAt(x, z, rs)` with the walk-blocking radius `rs` the new pile will have (`pileSolidRadius`), and `spotAround` searches in steps of 0.15 m (was 0.6) up to 3 m; `game/game` `layLeftovers` uses the same `blockedAt`; `game/mind` uses `gapFree` for the ring of upgrade piles (`homeNeed`) and lays the building pair `PAIR_HALF` (0.925) either side of the site, the second pile from beside the first. Consequences: `UP_AT` stays 2.1 but the ring of a kind is searched out to `UP_AT + 1.2`, so `UP_NEAR` is 4 (was 3); the arc's build pair stays within 2 m (a pair of driftwood piles stands at least 1.85 m apart). Nothing is moved that is already there (old saves keep their piles). Tests: `tests/walls.test.js`.

### Phase 2a: built (steps 1 to 3), and where it differs from the design below

Modules: `game/walk`, `game/fine`, `game/route` (new), `game/obstacles` (`campSolids`, `leantoPoint`; `solidObstacles` is now a thin callback whose `.camp` is the cached view), `game/path` (rewritten), `game/mind` (`walk`), `tests/helpers/routeWalk.js` (replaces `follow.js`), `tests/routes.test.js`, `tests/walk.test.js`; `tests/solid.test.js` and `tests/pathFollow.test.js` run on `routeWalk`. What differs from the design text below, and why:

- **Margins**: planning keeps 0.05 m (PLAN_MARGIN) from a circle, the follower 0.04 (FOLLOW_MARGIN), so a planned line is always visible to the follower and the fine nodes (0.06 outside) are usable. A line may start or end inside a circle's margin zone (outside the circle itself) as long as it does not come nearer than that end (a standing point 2 cm outside a fire place is a legal goal); a start inside a circle leaves it by a line that does not get nearer to its middle. If that finds no way out at all (wedged between overlapping piles) the connection is tried again with the old rule (any line out of the circles he stands in; movement slides him), then with trunks dear instead of closed.
- **Natural circles**: dear (x0.25) on grid edges as before, **closed** for the start and goal connections and the fine edges, and not clear for corner cutting. A start or goal connection past a trunk is used only when nothing else is left.
- **A goal inside a circle** (piles' centres, as `path.cost(p, pile)` asks) is aimed at the circle's rim, 0.12 m beyond it on the side he comes from; a goal in the very circle he stands in is cost 0. `game/mind` `walk` aims 0.04 m (was 0.12) outside the rim of any solid a target lies in (not only a solid's exact centre, and not when he stands in that circle): with the stop distance of 0.35 on top, 0.12 left him beyond `PILE_JOIN` (1 m from the pile's middle) too often and the wood he laid became new fragment piles (join rate in a 40-walk test 18 of 40 with 0.12 against 35 of 40 now; the old walk: 25 of 40). Standing points (step 4) replace this.
- **`path.clear`** also keeps to allowed ground (`allow`, checked every 0.75 m after the start): the follower's shortcuts must not cut across ground the exploring finder or the known-ground finder closed. `game/mind` starts no exploring trip from ground its finder does not allow (a climbing route), which the old start-cell logic did by accident.
- **The negative cache** works with `allowVersion()` (the mind passes `known.count`); without it a finder with an `allow` remembers no failure. `ban`/`banned` stay for the playtest bot only; the mind does not use them.
- **Follower**: a point is passed within 0.3 m (less than one step of 0.29 m, so he cannot step over it and turn back); the string is looked at again every 0.2 s or when the index moves; the first stall of a shortcut denies every shortcut from the current index (not one by one), the second closes the plan edge. A stall within 1.3 m of a tapped thing ends the walk as arrived (the old "lies against a trunk" rule, `near`). the stall time is 0.7 s (design 1.2 s: four stalls in a row then stay under the 3 s `stuckMax` limit of the lives), `stats.stuck` counts every stall (the old: every 4 s), `stats.nopath` and the events `nopath`/`stuck` keep their names; a walk fails after the fourth stall.
- **Not built**: ring nodes for natural circles (the lean-to matrix has no trunk stalls), the apron, `actionsAt`, standing points, the click-to-walk change.
- `tests/sit.test.js`: the amble's speed ceiling is `WALK_SPEED * PACE * 1.3` (was 1.1): the follower rounds a pile 4 to 6 cm off its rim and one step in two game days slid 24 % further along a rim.

### Phase 2b: built (steps 4 and 5, plus two fixes found on the way)

- **`game/game`**: `actionsAt(px, pz)` is the whole action rule evaluated as if he stood at (px, pz) (`actions()` is `actionsAt` at his feet; `lightable`, `buildable`, `upgradable`, `sleepable`, the planting spots and the wet test take the spot), `actionAt` the ring and arc for it, `offersAt(px, pz)` the flat list of what the button and arc would offer there. `game.stand` is the standing-point module. `buildable` (rule (b)) walks `wood.findAll(px, pz, BESIDE, 'pile')` nearest first (the larger pile among equals) and offers the first pile `siteFor` accepts with a dry site. The game follows a route (a private cached finder, no known-ground limit, `game/route`) for a click on a pile or fire (the spot is the old "beside" point 0.75 from the middle when he can stand there and the game offers the action for that object, else the cheapest standing point), for the walk of a light, build or upgrade, and for a plain click only when `finder.open` finds a camp solid on the straight line; a target with `routed: true` (the mind, the tests) is left to its own route; an unreachable target is cleared. Rule (a): `blockedAt` (keep-free region of every lean-to, pile solid 0.65) is given to `wood.drop`; `layLeftovers` moves what is left of the building piles when the lean-to is begun (nearest legal spot from 1.5 m out in steps of 0.25, the side away from the opening first, one pile per kind).
- **`game/wood`**: `findAll`, `joinTarget` (the pile `layKind` joins; `layKind` uses it), `drop(..., blockedAt)` and `spotAround` that looks 0.6 m further out, up to 3 m, when a spot was refused for the region (a spot refused for wetness alone is as before: none). **`game/leanto`**: `KEEP`, `keepFree(l)`.
- **`game/stand`** (new): as designed in section 4, kinds `take`, `add`, `light`, `feed`, `build`, `upgrade`, `nearPile`, `nearFire`; 24 angles, rings of 0.15 m, 0.06 m clear of every solid, offered at the spot and at 8 points 0.12 m round it; `choose` thins a long list to the 14 nearest and the nearest of 12 directions before `findAny`, all of it only when that finds no way. Cost: about 100 to 400 candidates, 1 to 6 ms a plan.
- **`game/mind`**: `goStand(kind, obj)` (arrival only when `stand.offered` at his real position; false is an honest unreachable), used by gather (take from a pile), `toDest` (add to a pile; feed a fire; an exact first-pile drop), lightFire (and `breakPair`), feedFire, fetchDried, toDrying, deliver, the build and upgrade errands, roastCrab (`nearFire`). The 0.04 rim aim and the fixed side points are gone; a plain walk whose goal lies in a solid goes to the nearest free spot (`openNear`). Piles have an identity for the list of things given up (`idOf`: they had none, so one failure used to mark every pile). Frond and stone piles are wider than the join distance (1.2 against 1.0): nothing can be added from outside, so the upgrade materials are laid as piles of their own on the ring `UP_AT` (2.1 m) round the lean-to's middle (outside the region, inside the 3 m the upgrades take from). The drying pile at a lean-to is `DRY_LT` 1.9 m straight behind it (it is outside the region and so no longer under the bare roof's 1.5 m; with side walls, 2.5 m, it is dry). Stock for tomorrow's fire never feeds a fire that still burns: it is a pile of its own 2 m from it.
- **One rule, walk and stepper**: `movement.stepAhead` (the probe 0.25 m ahead and the short step; a short step refused alone is walked at the pace of the probe) is the one test `stepPlayer` makes for each direction and `walk.stepperOpen` makes along a planned line every 0.05 m (the look-ahead cut at the end of the line); `terrainPace` asks it where the ground along the line is at least 0.85 steep. Cause of the seed-7 pocket at (-151.8, -63.5): a steep band under 0.25 m wide hid between two places of the 0.25 m lattice; the planner crossed it, and the stepper, standing in it with the short step landing in it too, was refused in every direction but back (stuck 45, now 4 and none at the pocket).
- **Sitting speed** (`tests/sit.test.js`, bound back to 1.1): the 1.24 came from `pushOut`'s ejection from inside a solid (a pile laid in front of him is a solid round his feet): the push put him on the rim, farther than the step he took (0.233 against 0.189 m). `stepPlayer` now caps an ejection at the step taken where `pushOut`'s own rule allows that point. The old 1.1 measured sliding along a rim; the jump was a different thing.

### 1. Two layers in one planner (`game/path`, new `game/fine`)

- **Terrain layer** (as today, cache restored): the 1.5 m grid. `isStand` and the edge factors (steps of 0.25 m with `stepFactor`, natural trunks/boulders make an edge four times dearer) are **terrain only** and are cached for the life of the finder. Nothing in the cache depends on the camp.
- **Camp view** (`game/obstacles`, new `campSolids(wood, fire, leantos)`): a read-only object `{ key, circles, near(x, z, reach), hit(a, b, margin), inside(x, z, margin) }` built from the same circles as today (pile core `pileSolidRadius` + `BODY`, fire `FIRE_R`, burnt places, the lean-to back and sides) and cached by `key`, a number hashed from kind, position (to 1 mm) and radius of every solid. `wood.version` is not used: it also changes when something is carried. The key is compared per call (a few microseconds for 30 to 60 solids). Circles live in a 3 m bucket hash. `game.movement.obstacles` becomes `camp.near`, so `stepPlayer` gets the same cache (today it rebuilds all circles on every call).
- **Grid cells and edges near the camp**: a cell whose middle lies inside a circle is closed; a grid edge whose segment passes closer than the circle's radius to any circle is closed. Both are checked on the fly through the bucket hash (a segment test against 0 to 4 circles), never cached. The cached terrain result is combined with them.
- **Fine layer** (`game/fine`): nodes that let the planner go round and into the camp where the grid is too coarse.
  - Piles, fires and burnt places: `n = ceil(2*pi*r / 0.55)` (7 to 9) nodes on a ring of radius `(r + 0.06) / cos(pi/n)`, so the chord between two neighbours stays 0.06 m outside the circle. `r` already includes `BODY`.
  - Lean-to (local frame, `f` along the heading, `r` across): inside `S (0, 0)` (the sleep point), gate `G (0.65, 0)` between the side tips, front `F (1.6, 0)`, and the four outer corners of the offset outline `(-1.16, +-1.28)` and `(1.01, +-1.28)` (wall circle radius 0.3 + 0.06 beyond the wall lines). Segments `S-G-F` and the outline between the corners are clear. The ring nodes of the wall circles are not generated.
  - A node covered by any solid (inside `r - 0.001`) or not standable is dropped.
  - Edges: between two nodes up to 7 m apart (`FINE_SPAN`) and from a node to every standable grid cell within 3 m (`FINE_LINK`), when the straight segment is free by the traversability rule below. Computed lazily when A* expands the node and memoised for the life of the fine layer.
- **Search**: one A* over `grid cells + fine nodes + the start and goal points`. Heuristic as today (octile in metres on coordinates, x1.1). `allow` (known ground) applies to cells and to fine nodes. A route is a list of points (`{ x, z }`, the middles of cells and the fine nodes) ending at the goal point itself.
- **Start and goal connections** (replace `startCell` and `nearestCell`): a point links to the 4 nearest standable cells within 4.5 m and to every fine node within 4.5 m, each only if the straight segment from the exact point is free. A point that already lies inside a circle (a pile laid under him, the wall zone of a lean-to being built) may leave it along any segment that does not get closer to that circle's middle (movement's escape rule), and no other. A point inside the lean-to links to `S`/`G`/`F` whatever the grid looks like. No connection means no route: the honest "unreachable".
- API: `find(from, to, { maxExp, closed })`, `findAny(from, goals[], opts)` (one search to the cheapest of several goal points, for standing points), `cost`, `clear(a, b, margin?)` (delegates to the traversability rule), `setCamp(camp)`. `ban` and the `retry` that cleared bans go away; the follower closes exact segments instead (3).

### 2. One traversability rule (`game/walk`, new, pure)

`edgeFactor(world, a, b, camp, opts) -> 0..1` is the only place that decides whether a straight line can be walked, used by the grid edges, fine edges, connections, `path.clear`, the follower and the standing points: the terrain check in steps of 0.25 m (`stepFactor`, 0 = wall or deep water), every dynamic circle (closed when the segment comes closer than `r + margin`, margin 0 for planning, 0.04 for the follower's corner cutting), and the natural circles (dear x4 when planning; "not clear" for corner cutting). It is the same circle geometry `pushOut` enforces, so **every planned segment is walkable**; a property test checks it on random camps. `game/path` and `game/route` import `game/walk`; `game/movement` stays the stepper.

### 3. One follower (`game/route`, new, pure state machine; replaces `mind.walk`'s following, `tests/helpers/follow.js`)

- `createRoute(finder, from, goal, { stop, arrived })` then `route.update(player, dt, busy) -> { target, final, state }` with `state` `'going' | 'arrived' | 'unreachable'`. No generators and no input object: `game/mind` sets `input.target` from `target`, the tests step `stepPlayer` with the same call. `arrived(player)` is `|goal - p| <= stop` or, for a standing point, `|point - p| <= 0.12 && action offered`.
- **Straight segments and string pulling**: the target is the furthest of the next 4 route points (at most 6 m ahead) that is visible from the player by `edgeFactor` with margin 0.04; the index never goes back. This replaces the "reached within 0.45 m / passed within 1.1 m" radii.
- **No progress** (under 0.15 m in 1.2 s while not busy; built as 0.7 s, see "Phase 2a: built"): close the exact blocked segment (the plan edge or, for a shortcut, that visibility pair) in the route's own `closed` set and replan from where he stands. At most 3 replans per route, then `'unreachable'`. No cell bans, no hand steering, no escape rule on top.
- **Replan on a camp change**: when `camp.key` changes, check the rest of the route against the new circles only (one pass of segment tests); replan when a segment is closed or the player is inside a new solid's margin. A pile laid behind him costs nothing.
- Used by `game/mind` (all walks, one thin `goTo` wrapper), `game/game` for clicks on a pile or fire (standing point plus route), and the tests. The player's plain click on the ground stays a straight intent as now (the player steers, water and slopes are his call), but when the straight line meets a camp solid the click also follows a route; decide in the build with the real-movement tests.

### 4. Action standing points (`game/stand`, new, pure)

- `standPoints(kind, object, ctx) -> [{ x, z }]` and `chooseStand(finder, from, points) -> { point, route } | null`. Kinds and objects: `take`/`add` (a pile), `feed` (a fire), `light` (a pile), `build` (a site or half-built lean-to), `upgrade` (a lean-to), plus `place` (the exact first-pile drop of `exactPileDrop`, unchanged).
- **Candidates**: 24 angles (15 degrees) x radii `rim + 0.03 + k * 0.15` up to the action's reach (pile: to the visible edge + `BESIDE`; fire: `FIRE_R + BESIDE`; lean-to: `UP_REACH` or `LEANTO_REACH` from its middle), `rim` being the solid radius (0.65 pile, 0.7 fire). Typically 50 to 170 candidates.
- **Filter**: standable (terrain), at least 0.06 m outside every solid, dry as `game` wants it, and **the intended action is offered**: `ctx.offersAt(x, z)` is the game's own rule evaluated at the hypothetical spot (see below), and also at 4 points 0.08 m round it (the follower's arrival tolerance and the slide of `pushOut` must not lose it), **and the intended object is the one that counts**: for `take` the pickup's `thing.pile` is the target (nearest by `wood.find`), for `add` `wood.joinTarget(x, z, kind)` is the target pile (the function `layKind` uses itself), for `feed` the action's `fire`, for `light` the action's `pile` with `light` not shadowed by `build`, for `build`/`upgrade` the site or lean-to.
- **Choice**: the cheapest by `findAny` (one search, all candidates as goals), tie broken by larger clearance from solids. `null` is "unreachable": the caller marks the object bad (as `markBad` does now) and goes on, instead of idling 4 s and trying the other sides.
- **Arrival**: only when the follower is within 0.12 m of the point and `offersAt(player)` is true. If the world changed meanwhile and it is false, one new choice; after two, unreachable.
- **The game's rule at a spot**: `game.actions()` is split into `actionsAt(px, pz)` (every `player.x/z`, `here()`, `lightable()`, `buildable()`, `upgradable()`, `sleepable()` takes the spot as a parameter) and `actions()` = `actionsAt(player.x, player.z)`; `game.offersAt(px, pz)` is the same list. One source of truth: when the rules change, the standing points change with them. Cost 16 microseconds a call, so a choice is about 1 to 3 ms, once per errand and not per frame.
- No change of any reach (`REACH` 0.55, `BESIDE` 1.2, `PILE_JOIN` 1.0, `UP_REACH` 2.2, `LEANTO_REACH` 1.7) and no change of any radius.

### 5. Camp changes and the local rebuild

- `camp.key` changes whenever a pile, fire, burnt place or lean-to is added, moved, removed or finished (and for frond/stone piles when their radius changes). The fine layer keeps its circles by an identity string (`kind:x:z`); on a new key it diffs old and new, removes the nodes and memoised edges of removed circles, adds the ring nodes of new ones, and drops only the memoised edges that involve a node within `FINE_SPAN` of a changed circle. Expected cost for one changed pile: 9 nodes against about 60 neighbours, under 0.5 ms; a full rebuild of a camp of 8 piles, a fire and a lean-to under 5 ms (to be measured in the build; a lazy rebuild on the next `find` is the fallback).
- Terrain results are never touched by a camp change. Restoring the cache is the main performance gain (see Evidence); the negative cache (same cells, same `camp.key` and known-ground count give the same null) stops the 3000 repeated failing searches of a shut-in life.

### Two camp rules (agreed 2026-11-09)

- **(a) The inside and the apron stay free of wood.** In the lean-to's frame the keep-free region `K` is the footprint `-1.1 <= f <= 0.95, |r| <= 1.25` (back wall and sides with their body-clear edge, the gap and the front tips) plus the **apron** `0.95 < f <= 1.5, |r| <= 1.0`. The apron ends at 1.5 on purpose: `RING_AT` puts the habit fire's pile at `f = 2.2`, whose solid reaches back to `f = 1.55`, so that pile stays legal. A pile is refused where its solid circle (radius 0.65) touches `K`. `game/leanto` exports `keepFree(l)` (frame, inside test for a circle) for every lean-to, finished or not. `wood.drop` takes a `blockedAt(x, z)` next to `wetAt`, and `spotAround` tries further out (`dist`, then +0.6 m steps up to +3 m, all 12 directions each) before giving up, so wood laid from the inside lands beside the lean-to; with no spot the wood stays in his arm (as with wet ground). **After a build**, `game/game` takes what is left of the two building piles (they stood between the two sites and usually overlap `K`) and lays it as one pile per kind (driftwood and branches never mix) at the nearest legal spot outside `K` and outside the walls, at least `PILE_NEXT` from other piles and dry, preferably at the side of the lean-to that is not its front. Piles already inside `K` in an older save stay where they are (never moved silently); the planner routes round them as before.
- **(b) Build is offered when any pile within reach holds 6 or more driftwood with a partner pile within 2 m.** `game.buildable()` takes every pile within `BESIDE` of its edge (a new `wood.findAll`), not only the nearest, and offers the first that `siteFor` accepts (nearest first, then the larger pile). `light` and `pickup` keep the nearest pile; the standing points pick spots where the intended pile is the nearest, so fragments no longer hide the pile (see 4).

### Data and complexity

- Camp: circles (about 20 per lean-to, 1 per pile or fire), bucket hash 3 m, key by hash. Fine layer: `nodes[]` (`x, z, owner, kind`), node bucket hash, `edges: Map<(i<<16)|j, factor>` memoised, about 100 nodes for a large camp. Route: array of points, index, `closed: Set` of segment keys.
- A* expansions are cells + fine nodes; near the camp each cell expansion adds a bucket lookup and 0 to 4 circle tests per edge. Targets: static cache gives a repeat find of 1 ms; a 120 m route stays at 25 ms cold, 300 m at 100 ms cold; a standing-point choice 1 to 3 ms; a camp change under 0.5 ms; the ten simulated days of seed 7 (52 s of path time today) at most half of it.
